What it is

Assess is a look at what you already have. The tools you pay for, the ones people actually use, where data gets retyped, and which handoffs break.

It usually turns up two things: software you are paying for and not using, and a manual step nobody flagged because it has always been done that way.

Who it’s for

  • Growing companies, where the thing that worked at ten people is straining at thirty.
  • Anyone about to buy something, who would rather find out first whether they already own it.

What you walk away with

  • A written picture of your current systems and how they connect.
  • A list of where the time actually goes, ranked by how much of it there is.
  • Specific findings, not a maturity score.

Why John

Both halves of the problem.

John Correia is a professor of management information systems who researches how people actually adopt technology. Seven peer-reviewed papers, in journals including MIS Quarterly, on why people take up a tool or quietly work around it.

He also builds. Dashboards, automations, web apps, and a spatial audio platform taken from an architecture plan to working software. He directs a student consulting group that runs like a small company, which means he spends his week handing work to people who have to run it without him. That is the same problem you have.

Assessment is measurement, and measuring how people use systems is his research area.

Example

A study of 35 university sports analytics programs: the data collected and filtered by hand, then turned into a dashboard you can sort, map, and compare school by school. The assessment was the work. The dashboard just made it readable.
